The Frictionless Decade

Three technologies showed up at once, all selling the same pitch: the reward, minus the grind. Coincidence, or something we should name?

Scroll to read · Charts animate as you go

Cheap, subsidized AI didn't arrive so much as seep in. Everywhere at once, free or nearly free, too convenient to refuse. GLP-1 shots followed the same script: don't build the discipline, just buy the outcome. Different industries, same move.

Take what used to demand months of grind and collapse it into a transaction. Pull the thread and more comes loose. Gig work instead of careers. Renting instead of owning. Subscriptions instead of anything you can actually hold. "You'll own nothing and be happy" stopped being a slogan to argue about and started looking like a description of where we already are.

That's the unsettling part. Nobody flipped a switch. There was no single moment to revolt against, just a temperature creeping up one comfortable degree at a time, each trade reasonable on its own, the water warm and pleasant right until it isn't. The frog doesn't jump because no single degree ever feels like the moment to jump.

I'll lay out the pattern with real numbers and let you decide: three unrelated trends, or one slow boil wearing three faces? I won't tell you the water's too hot, but would you notice if it was?

03 — Rented, Not Owned

The benefit is leased, not bought

Here's what the brochure skips. The long way deposits the reward in you — the fitness, the skill. The shortcut delivers the same payoff, but only while you keep paying. Stop and it reverts: weight comes back, capability evaporates because you never built the underlying skill. You're not buying the benefit. You're renting a better version of yourself.

Cancel the subscription. The upgrade gets repossessed.

05 — Coincidence or Playbook?

The same facts, read two ways

This is where honest people split. Nobody disputes the pattern. They dispute the cause. Two readings, stated as strongly as I can make them.

The case for a pattern

They're all headed the same direction.

Subscriptions instead of deeds. Injections instead of discipline. Prompts instead of apprenticeship. The arrows mostly point the same way: access without accumulation. Each benefit is leased, not owned, and reverts when payments stop. When separate forces all reward renting your life over building it, maybe the overlap is the story — even if nobody planned it.

"Own nothing" stops sounding like a slogan and starts sounding like a weather report.

The case it's just markets

Three booms that happen to overlap.

GLP-1s took off because the science finally worked. AI is cheap because investors are subsidizing a land grab. Renting rose because homes got unaffordable and rates locked owners in place. Each has a boring, well-documented driver. No coordination required.

Fast tech plus a strained economy was always going to produce curves that look alike.

About that phrase

"You'll own nothing and be happy" comes from a 2016 speculative essay by Danish politician Ida Auken, dramatized in a World Economic Forum video. Fact-checkers and the WEF say there is no policy to abolish private property; the "coordinated plan" version spread later as a meme. Good to know before you pick a side above.

The curves are real. So are the shortcuts. And the part where you keep none of it unless you keep paying. Whether that's a design or just a weird decade is a judgment call. I've given you my read of the facts. The rest is yours.
