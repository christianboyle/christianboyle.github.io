Stoic Design

January 24, 2026

When you can't trust the design process, what can you trust? 🪞

Addressing New Problems With Old Ideas

In the modern AI era, the "process" is no longer a reliable competitive advantage. AI has raised the baseline of what is possible, automating the labor of creation and the "middle" steps of production. However, human judgment remains the ceiling. Whether you are coding, designing, or navigating life, your value has shifted from following a manual to the ability to choose, curate, and reason.

1. The Evolution of Coding and Design

Traditional methodologies often taught practitioners to "trust the process"—a rigid, step-by-step series of instructions meant to guarantee results. In the AI era, this "IKEA-style" manual is becoming outdated.

  • From Process to Curation: Because tools allow non-designers to prototype faster than a human can write a problem statement, the human role shifts toward curation. Your value is in your ability to choose the right solution from a sea of AI-generated options.
  • The End Experience Over Artifacts: Users do not care about "process artifacts" like journey maps or research decks; they care about the end experience. Success is defined by high craft and "delight"—details that require deep human care and "ruthless" attention to detail.
  • Solution-First Thinking: While traditional design discouraged starting with a solution, AI allows for rapid prototyping that enables teams to "apply technology backwards" to solve problems.

2. Life and the Stoic Point of Leverage

The shift in professional work mirrors the core principles of Stoicism, which posits that the quality of our life depends entirely on our internal judgments.

  • Internal Leverage: You cannot control external outcomes like market moves or the speed of technological change. Your only point of leverage is your internal judgment: your intentions, efforts, and responses.
  • Stability through Interpretation: Good judgment determines whether uncertainty feels like danger or simply a lack of information, and whether criticism feels like an attack or useful data.

3. Redefining Intuition

Intuition is not guessing or "vibes"; in the AI era, it's a trained skill.

  • Trained Reason: Intuition is the ability to make reasoned judgments quickly based on deep experience. It's a shortcut that works because you know the subject area so well.
  • Internal Models: By constantly absorbing feedback from users, data, and technical constraints, you build an internal model of the world. This allows you to make "snap choices" that AI cannot replicate.

Mapping Stoic Exercises to the Modern Design Process
Stoic Exercise Stoic Description Mapping to Modern Design & AI
Examine Judgments Notice your interpretation of a situation before it becomes an "automatic" fact. Challenge the Artifact: Question if a persona or journey map is a truth or just a rigid requirement that doesn't serve the end experience.
Anchor to Your Control Value your own intention and effort rather than factors outside your control. Focus on High Craft: You cannot control AI's speed, but you can control your ruthless attention to detail and human delight.
Use Experience to Correct Judgment Be willing to throw away old opinions when they no longer fit reality. Solution-First Iteration: If a "problem-first" approach stalls, pivot. Use AI to see what is possible, then update your strategy based on current reality.
Practice Judgment Deliberately Treat every small decision as training to make reasoned decisions quickly. Build "Trained" Intuition: Use constant feedback to build an internal model, allowing for "snap choices" that non-experts cannot make.
Discard the Manual Focus on present reality rather than a fixed script. "Chart the Map" Every Time: Stop "worshipping" the process and trust yourself to skip or invent steps that fit the unique constraints of each project.

Success in the modern era requires moving away from "trusting the process" and toward trusting yourself.

  1. Judgment is the Ceiling: AI raises the floor, but your intuition determines the final quality.
  2. Act Rightly: Focus on your response to a changing industry rather than arguing with the "calls" made by the market or technology.
  3. Embrace Vulnerability: It is empowering and vulnerable to rely on your own experience to decide which tools to wield and which rules to break.

In both coding and life, when your judgments are correct, you'll do well. Get the judgment right, and you'll find the best path forward—even when the circumstances are messy.
